Whose Demand? Whose Earth?

Relational perspectives and the logic of overshoot.

We hold two ideas together in this module: overshoot — the system math of using nature faster than it regenerates — and intersectional environmentalism — the relational lens that asks whose history, whose demand, whose harm. Both are needed. Neither is complete on its own.

Unit Framing Question

"How does viewing climate change as a complex adaptive system reshape the way we learn, teach, and respond to it?"

In the Complex Adaptive Systems module, we mapped climate change as a complex adaptive system with planetary limits. We saw that seven of nine planetary boundaries have already been crossed. But "humanity" did not cross those boundaries evenly. This module moves from system structure to system relations — asking who is doing most of the demanding, who is doing most of the absorbing, and whose voices the environmental movement has centered or erased along the way.

Module Overview

From system structure to system relations

Knowing that planetary boundaries exist doesn't tell us who crossed them, or why. This module connects the system math of overshoot with the relational lens of intersectional environmentalism — because neither is complete on its own.

This module comes with one required reading and one embedded video — they're linked right where you need them, so you're not juggling tabs. Going through the module first means the reading and video will click faster, since you'll already recognize the ideas. All in, plan for about 60 minutes. Bring one question that stayed with you to Thursday's class.
